Dealing With AC Leak Water Damage in Wilmington

All day long an AC system wrings humidity from indoor air, and the resulting water needs a way out. A pan under the coil catches the drips, and from there a thin pipe empties the pan. When that line clogs with algae and dust, the pan fills and spills over. Attic and closet air handlers send the spill directly into the surrounding ceiling, walls or flooring.

A slow drip causes more harm than most homeowners would guess. The water wicks into drywall, soaks the platform under the unit and creeps under flooring, often with mold already started by the time a stain shows. We find how far the moisture spread, dry the structure and repair what was damaged. Clearing the drain or repairing the unit remains a job for an HVAC technician, and our schedule can fit around theirs.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Turn Off the ACNo more condensate forms once the AC is turned off at the thermostat. Where the wiring on the air handler is getting wet, the breaker should also go off.
  2. Avoid Wet Wiring and LightsUntil that area's power has been shut off, keep your hands away from the unit and from any outlet or ceiling light with water in or near it.
  3. Clear Water From the PanUse a wet-vac or rags to take the standing water out of the pan and stop the spill, provided the pan is safe to reach. Skip this step if it means crossing a soaked attic.
  4. Dry the Easy SpotsMove clothes, boxes and furniture back from the stained area or the closet, catch ceiling drips in a bucket, wipe up hard floors and blot the carpet.
  5. Call Both TradesAn HVAC technician should clear the drain line, and our team should find out where the water went. Until that repair is done, the unit stays off.
The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • Bare refrigerant lines or ducts that sweat in humid weather and drip on the ceiling.
  • A condensate drain line clogged with algae, dust and slime from the coil.
  • Ice on the evaporator coil melting all at once and overwhelming the pan.
  • Units that rely on a condensate pump, when that pump quits or gets unplugged.
  • Water escaping from a drain pan that has cracked, rusted through or tilted.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Closet Walls and Platform

Many closet installations put the air handler on a wood platform, with the return box below it. Water rots that platform and wicks up the surrounding drywall, where it stays hidden behind the unit.

Insulation Around the Unit

Batts or blown-in material next to the pan absorb the leak and press that moisture onto the ceiling. Wet insulation loses its value and often has to be replaced.

Baseboards and Door Trim

Baseboards and door casings near the closet draw water up from the floor. Fiberboard trim swells and crumbles, so it is usually replaced, not dried.

Mold Around the Air Handler

Within 24 to 48 hours mold can start on wet wood and drywall next to the unit, and the return may draw spores into the ducts and around the home.

Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

With AC leaks, insurers mostly look at how long the water was running. Water damage is paid for under many homeowners policies when the overflow was sudden and you responded as soon as you discovered it. If the adjuster decides the drip went on for weeks or months, it may be treated as a gradual leak or a maintenance issue and denied. Either way, the cost of fixing the AC unit is typically left out. Check your policy, and we can supply readings and photos for the claim.

How We Fix It

Our AC Leak Water Damage Process

1

Measure the Spread

With thermal imaging and moisture meters, our team checks the unit, the adjacent walls or ceiling and the floor beyond the closet, so wet materials are found even where no stain shows.

2

Extract and Open Up

Our extractors pull water out of the pan, the wood platform and any carpet. Saturated or moldy insulation and drywall are removed, which opens the cavity to dry air.

3

Dry the Structure

Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on for a few days, usually 3 to 5, and we log the moisture readings. Machines are positioned to leave the HVAC technician a clear path to the unit.

4

Sanitize and Rebuild

Affected surfaces get an antimicrobial treatment, because condensate that has sat for days is rarely clean. Then we replace insulation, drywall, baseboard and flooring and repaint so the area matches.
