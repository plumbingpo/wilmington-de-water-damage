Wet Carpet Drying: What Wilmington Homeowners Should Know

A wet carpet is hiding a lot more water than you can see. Often the pad is full of water and the subfloor is wet beneath it, even when the fibers you touch feel only slightly damp. Towels and a shop vacuum reach the top layer and little else. In Wilmington, we pull the water out, take moisture meter readings on each layer, and place drying equipment that works down to the subfloor.

Saving a carpet comes down to how long it stayed wet and whether the water that soaked it was clean. Carpet soaked by a clean supply line can usually be dried in place if we get to it quickly. With an appliance leak, which is gray water, we often discard the pad, then clean and treat the carpet. Both layers, carpet and pad, are removed when the water was sewage or came in from outside flooding.

Signs to Look For

  • Ripples or loose spots where the carpet has let go of the tack strip
  • Backing that separates from the carpet when you lift a corner to look
  • A rust colored line at the wall edge, a sign of wet tack strip
  • A squishy feel under your feet, or wet socks after walking across the room
  • As the carpet dries, yellowish or brown stains wick to the top of the pile
  • A sour, musty smell that gets stronger when the room is closed up
If Nothing Is Done

The Damage Timeline

First hours

Water Moves Outward

Water travels through the pad far beyond the patch you can see. It slips beneath walls into neighboring rooms while the tack strip begins to absorb it.

24 to 48 hours

Smell and Mold Start

Bacteria in the damp pad start producing that sour wet carpet smell. Dust held down in the fibers and the carpet backing both give mold a place to begin.

Days 3 through 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

Ripples appear and the carpet delaminates because the latex in its backing is breaking down. The subfloor swells and the tack strip nails begin to rust.

After a week

Too Late to Save

By now the carpet and pad usually have to come out. Mold has often reached the subfloor and the bottom of the drywall, so the job grows.

Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Wet Wall Bottoms

The base of each wall is where water moving beneath the carpet ends up. Because carpet covers the edge, no one sees the drywall and baseboards drawing the water upward.

Swollen Subfloor

Left wet, the OSB or particleboard beneath a carpet swells and then crumbles. The soft spots, squeaking and lifted seams appear weeks afterward, when nobody remembers the spill.

Mold Under the Carpet

Growth usually starts on the underside of the carpet, where it is dark and damp. The odor arrives a long time ahead of any visible sign on top.

Stains From Furniture

Furniture legs sitting on wet carpet release wood stain and rust into the fibers. The furniture also draws up water and swells, and the marks it leaves often never come out.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Find the Edges

We trace how far the water traveled with moisture meters and thermal imaging, including under walls and into closets. We also identify the water category, because that decides what can stay.

2

Extract the Water

Weighted extraction tools press water out of the carpet and the pad together. This removes far more than a wand alone, and it shortens the drying time that follows.

3

Dry All the Layers

The carpet is either pulled back so the pad can go, or left down with air blown beneath it, whichever the job needs. Our air movers and dehumidifiers keep going until meters show a dry carpet and a dry subfloor.

4

Clean and Reinstall

Last, we apply antimicrobial to carpet and subfloor if they need it, replace any pad that was taken out, stretch the carpet back over the tack strip, and give it a cleaning.
