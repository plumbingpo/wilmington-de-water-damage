Dealing With Roof Leak Water Damage in Wilmington

A roof leak rarely starts where the stain appears. A loose shingle, a failed flashing joint or an aging seal around a skylight lets rain in, and it travels across the roof deck and framing until it falls into the insulation. Often the attic has been damp for some time before any brown mark reaches the room below. We find the full wet area and dry it out.

Our trade is water damage restoration, and roofing is a separate one. Our job is the inside of the house: pulling out soaked insulation, drying the framing and drywall, treating for mold and putting the ceiling back together. When the roof is still letting water through, a temporary tarp is something we can install, and we will coordinate with your roofer so the repair comes before the ceiling is closed.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Shut Off Wet CircuitsIf water is dripping through a light fixture, a ceiling fan or near an outlet, switch off that circuit at the breaker and leave the fixture alone.
  2. Catch the DripsPut a pan or bucket beneath each drip, and shift rugs, electronics and furniture clear of the wet area. Lay towels or plastic sheeting down to protect the floor.
  3. Relieve a Ceiling BulgeIf the ceiling is holding a pocket of water, stand to one side and poke a small hole at the lowest point so it drains into a bucket.
  4. Leave the Roof AloneClimbing on slick shingles or crawling around a dark attic is not worth the risk. Just observe the entry point from the room below, then hand the job to a trained crew.
  5. Document What You SeeBefore anything is moved or discarded, get video and photos of the attic, the ceiling and damaged items, and then phone us so drying can begin.
The Usual Culprits

What Causes It

  • A fallen limb or large hail that punctures or bruises the roof covering.
  • Flashing that has rusted, lifted or pulled away around chimneys, vents and walls.
  • A valley liner that has worn through at the point where the most runoff passes.
  • Popped or exposed roofing nails, each one a tiny opening for rain.
  • Wind-torn, curling or split shingles that leave the underlayment open to rain.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Insulation in the Attic

Wet insulation mats down, stops insulating and holds water against the ceiling drywall for days. In some cases fiberglass batts dry out and stay. Soaked cellulose usually has to be removed.

Mold in the Attic

Damp framing, paper-faced drywall, warmth and darkness give mold what it needs. Within 24 to 48 hours it can start growing, then move across the bottom of the roof deck.

Drywall Ceilings

Staining, sagging and loss of strength follow when drywall is not dried. Joints open, tape peels, and a waterlogged piece may fall without much notice.

Lights and Wiring

Leak water heads for openings in the ceiling, such as recessed lights, fan boxes and junction boxes. Keep that circuit off until someone has inspected it, since damp wiring may corrode or short out.

Our Process

Our Roof Leak Water Damage Process

1

Trace the Leak Path

Moisture meters and a thermal camera let us follow the rain through the attic, the ceilings and the walls, so spots that look dry from underneath are not missed.

2

Tarp and Protect

If the roof is still open to the weather, we can install a temporary tarp or coordinate with your roofer. Floors and belongings below the wet ceiling are covered as well.

3

Tear Out and Dry

Unsalvageable drywall and insulation come out, and dehumidifiers and air movers go into the attic space and each room under it, with readings taken until the framing has dried.

4

Treat and Rebuild

Exposed wood gets an antimicrobial treatment where needed. Once the roof repair is done, we replace insulation, hang and finish new drywall, then prime and paint to match the ceiling.

Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

Homeowners policies often cover interior water damage when a roof leak is sudden and accidental, such as wind tearing off shingles or a limb puncturing the roof. Leaks blamed on age, wear or skipped maintenance are usually denied, and the roof repair itself may be handled differently from the interior. Our team gives your adjuster photos and moisture readings, yet what is covered comes down to your policy, so check it or talk to your agent.
