Dealing With Water Heater Leak Cleanup in Wilmington

A water heater holds 40 to 80 gallons, but that is not the limit of the leak. Your supply line keeps feeding the tank, which means it fills back up just as quickly as water escapes through a rusted bottom. Until someone closes the cold water valve, it keeps running. The location of the heater sets the damage. It may be a hallway closet, a garage floor or, the worst case, an attic over finished rooms.

Our Wilmington team extracts the water, then looks at what surrounded the heater. The platform beneath the tank and the base of the closet walls are usually soaked. Garage leaks wet whatever is stored on the floor, along with the wall the garage shares with the house. Water from an attic unit soaks through insulation, then through the ceiling. Our crew takes readings in every area and sets drying equipment to bring it back to normal.

Before We Arrive

  1. Turn Off the Heat SourceIf the heater is electric, turn its breaker off. With a gas heater, the knob on the control goes to the off position. A burner or heating element must not stay on once the tank is empty.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveThe cold line over the tank has a valve, and it closes with a clockwise turn. When that valve leaks or will not move, close the main water valve to the house.
  3. Watch for Hot WaterTank water is hot enough to scald. Let pooled water cool off before anyone steps in it, and keep kids and pets out of the area.
  4. Empty the Tank OutsideWhen the drain valve is within reach, connect a garden hose and lead it to a floor drain or outdoors, which keeps the rest of the water off the floor.
  5. Move Stored ItemsAnything absorbent, including boxes and rugs, should come up from the wet area. Cardboard and fabric soak up water quickly and hold it against walls and concrete.
Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • A blocked or rusted drain pan under an attic or closet heater that overflows instead of draining.
  • A worn anode rod that no longer protects the tank lining from rust.
  • A missing expansion tank or a failed thermostat that lets too much pressure build inside.
  • Loose plumbing connections left after a recent installation or repair.
  • An aging steel tank whose bottom is eaten through by corrosion and sediment.
Why Speed Matters

What the Water Damages

Stand and Subfloor Damage

The wooden stand or subfloor under the tank takes the most water. Rot can weaken it, a real concern once a full new tank sits on top.

Closet Walls

Drywall surrounding a heater closet inside the house wicks water up and lets it through to the neighboring rooms. The lower wall remains wet on the inside, and the baseboards swell.

Adjacent Room Flooring

From a heater closet, the leak slips beneath the door and reaches hallway hardwood, laminate or carpet. Heat makes it worse, with quicker swelling of wood boards and carpet backing that comes apart.

Items Stored Near the Heater

The first casualties are usually the furniture, boxes and keepsakes stored around the heater in a garage or closet. Cardboard falls apart, and wet contents can mold if they are not dried or discarded quickly.

What We Do

Our Water Heater Leak Cleanup Process

1

Remove Standing Water

Our crew extracts the water standing in the closet, the garage or on the floor, plus the overflow pan and the cavity above the ceiling on attic jobs, so that materials around it quit absorbing it.

2

Inspect the Surrounding Area

We go over the wall bases, the platform, any ceiling underneath and the adjoining rooms with thermal imaging and moisture meters, which shows how far the wet area extends.

3

Remove Wet Material

Damaged baseboards, drywall that has started to crumble and saturated insulation all get removed. Attic jobs include removing wet insulation to expose the joists and the ceiling drywall for drying.

4

Drying and Moisture Readings

Drying usually takes 3 to 5 days, and dehumidifiers and air movers run the whole time. Moisture readings are recorded, and you hear from us once the space can take the new heater.

Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and Water Heater Leak Cleanup

When a tank fails suddenly, the water damage to floors, walls and belongings is often covered as sudden and accidental. Usually the unit is left out of the claim, as age related wear is viewed as a maintenance matter. Rust trails, old staining or other evidence that the leak was long-term can lead an adjuster to reduce or deny the claim, since gradual leaks usually fall outside coverage. Until the claim is closed, hold on to the old heater. Photograph it along with the damage, and review your policy.

Pricing

What Drives the Price

  • The length of time the tank went on refilling until someone closed the supply valve.
  • Where the heater sits, with attic and interior closet leaks costing more than garage leaks.
  • Whether the platform or subfloor under the tank has to be rebuilt before a new heater goes in.
  • How much insulation, ceiling or wall drywall needs to be taken out.
