Warning Signs for Storm Damage Repair

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Damaged or displaced flashing around roof penetrations, valleys or edges
  • Flooding in lower levels because drainage systems were overwhelmed
  • Gutters or downspouts torn loose, bent or dumping water against the house
  • After a weather event, insulation inside walls or overhead feels soaked
  • Puddles near skylights, beside a chimney or on the attic floor
  • Shingles that are gone, cracked or curled up when you look from the yard
  • Swollen ceiling panels or blistered, peeling paint in the days after a storm

Why Professional Storm Damage Repair Matters

The repairs a storm leaves behind are tangled together, and sorting them out correctly calls for an experienced restoration crew. What seems like a small leak indoors can hide widespread water damage in wall cavities, roof decking and insulation, which turns into mold and structural decay unless it is identified and dried properly. Professional restoration technicians use thermal imaging and moisture meters to trace water paths and identify every affected area.

Tarping and boarding up only work when they are done with the right technique. If a tarp is fastened poorly, it can direct water where you do not want it or give out when the wind picks up. We have done a lot of storm mitigation, and we use suitable materials and methods so the temporary protection holds until permanent repairs are on the calendar.

Storm Damage Repair Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Install impact-resistant windows or storm shutters if you live in a hurricane-prone area
  • Extend downspouts and slope the soil so runoff moves away from the foundation
  • Keep gutters and downspouts clear so water drains properly in heavy rain
  • Trim trees near your home to lower the risk of wind-driven branch damage
  • Good attic insulation and ventilation stop ice dams from forming along the roof edge
  • Before storm season each year, get the roof inspected and its problem areas repaired
