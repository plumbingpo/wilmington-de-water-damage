Sump Pump Failure Cleanup Help in Wilmington

Sump pumps do a single task, yet they commonly quit right when that task matters. Groundwater rises in a big storm and pours into the pit, right as the electricity fails, the float jams or the inflow outruns the pump. The pit overflows and the basement floor starts to flood. Nothing stops it until some pump is working again.

Do not think of it as clean water. Soil, silt, bacteria and the contents of the drain tile travel with it, so our team rates it no better than Category 2, and Category 3 when it has stood for a while or sewage is involved. For the job, this means soaked porous items such as drywall and carpet pad are usually discarded, and what stays gets washed and disinfected first, then dried.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Keep Out of the BasementWith the electricity still on, a flooded basement is not safe to walk into. The furnace, wall outlets or the cord of the pump itself could be in contact with it.
  2. Shut Off Basement PowerBasement breakers should be flipped off, provided you can get to the panel without standing in water. A panel surrounded by water is a job for the power company.
  3. Check the Pump SafelyLook to see whether the pump came unplugged, its outlet tripped or the float is pinned against the pit wall. Reaching into the pit or the water is not safe.
  4. Assume the Water Is DirtyChildren and pets stay on the upper floor, anyone who has to enter wears rubber boots and gloves, and hands get washed after contact with anything wet.
  5. Move Valuables and CallCarry dry valuables upstairs, photograph the water level and the pump, then call us. Waiting for it to recede without help is a mistake.
Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and Sump Pump Failure Cleanup

Sump pump losses are widely misunderstood when it comes to insurance. Standard homeowners coverage generally leaves out sump overflow and backup, while flood coverage is bought separately and follows other rules. Coverage often exists only if you added a water backup or sump overflow endorsement, and those carry their own limits. Your declarations page will list it, or your agent can tell you. We record the pump failure, the water level and the damage so your claim file is complete.

The Usual Culprits

How It Usually Starts

  • A power outage during the storm with no battery backup on the pump.
  • Dirt and small rocks washed into the basin that lock up the pump.
  • More water flowing into the pit than an undersized pump can move.
  • A tripped outlet or breaker, or a plug knocked loose, leaving the pump without power.
  • Years of on and off cycling finally burning out an aging motor.
What Is at Risk

What the Water Damages

Basement Floor Coverings

Carpet and pad soaked by groundwater cannot be cleaned to a safe standard and are removed. Laminate and vinyl plank trap silty water underneath and usually come up too.

Low Electrical Outlets

Wall outlets near the floor are sometimes submerged in a sump flood. Have an electrician look at wet receptacles and cable before power is restored to them.

Boxes and Stored Items

Books, cloth, boxes and wood furniture legs take in dirty water within a short time. Within days mold shows up on absorbent things that were in it, and they are difficult to disinfect.

Bottom Plates and Studs

Framing lumber at floor level takes on groundwater before anything else in the wall. Once the drywall is off, the plates and studs are washed, disinfected and dried.

What We Do

Our Sump Pump Failure Cleanup Process

1

Pump Out the Basement

Our crew arrives with extractors and pumps, which means your dead sump is not needed. Low spots, the area by the pit and the rest of the floor are cleared of water and silt.

2

Remove Contaminated Materials

Wearing protective gear, we take out the carpet, pad, wet drywall and insulation that the groundwater touched, bag it and haul it away so clean framing and concrete are exposed.

3

Wash and Disinfect

We wash the framing, the slab and the contents that are staying, then apply an antimicrobial. Drying comes afterward, because fans on a dirty basement would blow contaminants everywhere.

4

Dry and Confirm

Typically for 3 to 5 days, air movers and dehumidifiers stay in place, until the concrete and framing read normal on a moisture meter. A working pump should be back in the pit before our equipment leaves.
