Water Damage We See Most in Dover

Six kinds of calls make up the bulk of our work for Dover households. They are all common, and with every one of them waiting adds to the repair.

Mold: Most mold growth is the follow-up to an earlier leak that seemed to be taken care of. Surfaces dry with towels and a box fan, yet the pad, subfloor and inside of the wall hold moisture. Since colonies can form in 24 to 48 hours, the first hint is often an earthy smell or speckled drywall weeks later.

Hoses: The braided or rubber lines behind toilets, sinks and washing machines are under pressure all day. When one splits, it sprays until the water is turned off, which can mean hours if nobody is home. Replacing old connectors and knowing where your shutoff sits are the cheapest protection available.

Pumps: Sump pumps, where a home has one, tend to fail at the worst moment after years of running without attention. What we usually see is a jammed float, a motor that has worn out or a breaker that tripped. The result is several inches of water over the lowest floor, soaking stored boxes, flooring and the bottom of the walls. A battery backup and a quick test before the rainy part of the year go a long way.

Toilets: Among bathroom emergencies in Dover, the toilet that will not stop rising tops the list. It matters where the water came from, because tank or supply water is clean and used bowl water is not. In both cases it seeps beneath cabinets and flooring edges, and a moisture meter shows how far it went.

Cooling: Your air conditioner drains condensation through a line that most owners never see. If the line plugs or the pan rusts through, water drips into the ceiling or the floor around the unit. The leak is gradual, so drywall and insulation may be soaked long before you spot a mark. Having the line flushed each year, before the cooling season, prevents most of these.

Flooding: A long, heavy rain can overwhelm street drains and send water across the yard and into the house. Garages, walkout basements and ground-floor rooms at the low side of the lot flood first. We pump it out, discard materials that cannot be sanitized and keep equipment running until moisture readings are back to normal.

The right first steps change with the type of water on your floor. Clean water from a supply pipe or a tap left running is the least risky. You can walk in it with shoes on, soak it up, and move belongings out of it. Discharge from appliances, such as a dishwasher or washer, carries more bacteria, and gloves and hand washing are sensible. Anything from a sewer, or water that came in from outside, is hazardous, so shut the room and stay away from it.

Whatever the type, a few things help our Dover crew start faster. Make room for the truck near the entrance and clear the route from the front door to the damage. Note the time you discovered the problem and your best guess at the source. Find your insurance policy number if it is easy to get to.

Insurance and Water Damage: A Guide for Dover Owners

A claim is decided on what can be shown, which makes documentation the most useful tool a Dover owner has. Begin collecting it before the cleanup and before anything goes in the trash.

Photograph the source of the water, such as the split hose or the failed fitting, and keep the broken part if you can. Insurers often draw a line between sudden damage, like a burst pipe, and damage from a slow leak or outside flooding.
