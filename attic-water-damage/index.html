Attic Water Damage in Wilmington

Water reaches an attic from two directions. Worn out shingles, flashing that has failed, and the seals around chimneys and vents all let rain in from overhead. From below, household moisture rises and condenses on the cold roof sheathing if attic ventilation is poor. Either way, hardly anyone goes up there to look. In Wilmington, we inspect the attic, track down the source, and get it dry.

Wet insulation is the heart of most attic jobs. Heating and cooling bills go up because cellulose and fiberglass alike give up R-value when wet. The bigger problem is that for weeks, saturated insulation holds moisture on the drywall of the ceiling and on the framing. We remove what is saturated, dry the wood with air movers and dehumidifiers, and treat any mold on the rafters or sheathing.

What It Looks Like

  • Stains or drips where a light fixture or exhaust fan meets the ceiling
  • Dripping, rusted or water marked ducts and metal hardware up in the attic
  • After rain, a stain or brown ring on a top floor ceiling that looks worse
  • Streaks or dark staining on the roof deck's underside and on the rafters
  • Closets on the top floor, or the attic, that smell musty
  • An upstairs ceiling where the paint has blistered or started to peel
Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Wet Wiring

An attic contains recessed light cans, junction boxes and runs of cable. Water in or on them is a shock and fire risk, so stay out until the power is checked.

A Falling Ceiling

Soaked insulation carries a great deal of weight. The ceiling drywall under it weakens where it is fastened, and it can give way suddenly and bring the wet insulation along.

Leaks From Attic Equipment

Many attics hold an air handler, a drain pan and condensate lines. For weeks an overflowing pan or blocked drain can drip into insulation, and there is no storm to tip you off.

Lost Insulation Value

Insulation that is damp and compressed barely insulates, and drying may not bring it back. The rooms beneath that section get colder or hotter, and you pay more for energy.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Inspect and Trace

To learn whether you have a leak, condensation or the two together, we go over the roof deck, the vents, the exhaust ducts and each flashing point. Its full reach is mapped with a thermal camera and meter readings.

2

Take Out Wet Insulation

Saturated or moldy insulation is bagged and removed so the wood and drywall under it can dry. Rooms under the attic are protected, and the route in and out stays contained.

3

Dry the Framing

Air movers and dehumidifiers bring the rafters, joists and sheathing back to normal moisture levels. An antimicrobial goes on after any visible mold has been cleaned from the wood.

You are shown the ventilation or roof problem to be corrected, and our findings can go to your roofer. When the attic has stayed dry, the R-value is restored with new insulation.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

The first storm

Insulation Soaks It Up

The insulation catches and absorbs whatever rain gets by the roofing. With no sign inside the house, nobody notices the leak and the roof stays unrepaired.

24 to 48 hours

Mold Can Start

In a still, warm attic, paper facing and wood that stay damp give mold ideal conditions. Growth can begin on the sheathing and on the back of the ceiling drywall.

Weeks later

Stains Come Through

After repeated wetting, the insulation is saturated and water reaches the ceiling. In the room underneath, brown rings show up and the drywall may begin sagging.

Months later

Wood Starts Rotting

Sheathing delaminates and loses its hold on the roofing nails. Large areas of roof decking are covered in mold, while rafters that never dry begin to decay.
