Wall and Drywall Water Damage in Wilmington

A sheet of drywall behaves much as a wick does. Once the base of a wall gets wet, moisture climbs through the paper and gypsum, commonly a foot or more higher than the water stood on the floor. The painted face can look and feel normal while the back side and the insulation behind it are soaked. For that reason our team relies on moisture meter readings rather than appearance.

Not every wet wall has to be torn out. Firm panels wetted by clean water are frequently saved, in some cases with the baseboard taken off and small openings drilled so air can move inside the wall. When the wall is soft, crumbling, moldy or was soaked by dirty water, we make a flood cut, a straight line above the damage, and remove everything below it.

Warning Signs

  • Carpet edges or floorboards wet and discolored right next to the wall
  • A tide line or discoloration running along the bottom of the wall
  • Soft or crumbly spots you notice when pushing lightly on the wall
  • A musty smell that is stronger near one wall or outlet
  • Dark spots or fuzzy growth at corners and behind furniture
  • Paint or wallpaper that bubbles, wrinkles or peels near the floor
How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Find Every Wet Area

We scan each wall with a moisture meter and thermal camera and mark how high and how far the water traveled, including the opposite side and adjoining rooms.

2

Decide What Stays

You get a straight answer on what can be dried where it is and what must be removed, judged by the category of water, the time it sat and the firmness of the drywall.

3

Cut or Ventilate

Walls we are keeping have their baseboards removed so air can be pushed into the cavity. On sections coming out, a straight flood cut is made and soaked insulation is removed.

4

Drying and Reconstruction

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until studs and remaining drywall read dry, typically 3 to 5 days. After that comes insulation, new drywall hung and finished, trim put back and paint.

The Damage Timeline

First hours

Water Starts Climbing

The bottom edge of the drywall takes in water from the floor and it begins moving up. Baseboards and the bottom plate take on moisture.

Hours 24 to 48

Swelling and Blisters

You may see blistered paint and swollen trim as the damp area moves up the wall. Inside the cavity, the paper face becomes a place mold can begin.

Days 3 through 7

Drywall Breaks Down

Paper peels from a core that is no longer firm. Insulation slumps inside the wall, odor builds and mold spreads across the hidden back side.

Past one week

Damage Reaches the Studs

Studs and plates stay wet long enough to grow mold and begin to decay. The amount of removal and reconstruction grows considerably.

What You Cannot See

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Spread Into Adjoining Rooms

The neighboring room or closet gets wet as water travels below the wall plate. Every wall involved is tested on its two faces, and so is the adjacent flooring.

Wet Sheathing Outside

Outside walls have sheathing past the insulation that can also get soaked. That layer dries slowly and is checked before the wall is closed.

Wet Outlets and Wiring

Water pools at the same height as most receptacles and cable runs. Until that circuit is inspected, avoid plugging anything into the affected wall.

Hidden Behind Cabinets

Wet drywall behind cabinets, vanities and built-ins gets no airflow and is easy to overlook. We take readings through toe kicks and from the opposite side to find it.
