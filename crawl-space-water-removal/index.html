Crawl Space Water Removal in Wilmington

Water under the house is easy to miss. The usual clues are chilly, clammy floors, an earthy odor rising from the registers, or a pool of water waiting behind the access door. At that point the batts are typically saturated and the framing overhead has sat damp for several days. We pump the space dry, remove the ruined material and get the wood dried while it is still sound.

Pumping is just half of the work. Because lumber under a floor is slow to soak and slow to dry, our crew tracks its moisture content with a meter and runs dehumidification for the 3 to 5 days it normally takes to bring the readings down. Finding the cause matters just as much, since a dripping water line, a failed drain or groundwater from a storm will flood the area again unless it is addressed.

Signs to Look For

  • A musty, earthy smell that is strongest near the floor vents
  • Moisture loving pests such as camel crickets and silverfish appearing indoors
  • Steel posts, hangers for pipes and duct straps showing orange rust
  • A first floor with a cool, moist feel and some give when you walk on it
  • Dark water marks and chalky deposits partway up the block or concrete
  • Edges of the floorboards lifting into a washboard pattern in the rooms above
The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

Day one

Water Pools Below

Low areas fill first while the ground underneath takes on water. Insulation starts wicking it up and humidity under the floor climbs fast.

24 to 48 hours

Early Mold Growth

Paper faced insulation and moist joists are where mold is able to appear first. The weight of the water drags the batts loose from the underside of the subfloor.

Day 3 to 7

Dampness Rises Indoors

Damp air rises into the living space. You might see floorboards cupping and notice a musty odor while the framing continues to soak.

After a week

Wood Starts Rotting

Wood that stays wet begins to decay. Sill plates and joists lose firmness, nails and hangers corrode, and termites along with other pests move toward the moisture.

Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Settling Under the House

When the ground stays soaked, footings may lose support and piers can tilt or sink. Sloping floors or sticking doors in the rooms above may point to this.

Wet Wiring and Ducts

Electrical runs, junction boxes and ductwork pass through most crawl spaces. Submerged wiring is a shock hazard and soaked duct insulation spreads musty air through the system.

Contaminated Water

Water under a house is often not clean. If it came from a drain pipe or from waste laden groundwater it is rated Category 2 or 3, so we suit up and disinfect.

Weakened Floor Framing

Decay tends to begin along the upper edge of the framing or where it meets the sill, out of view from the access opening. We probe the wood to find soft spots.

Our Process

Our Crawl Space Water Removal Process

1

Safety Check and Inspection

We check for electrical hazards, sagging ducts and contaminated water before anyone crawls in. Next comes charting the extent of the water and metering the joists and subfloor for moisture.

2

Remove Standing Water

Standing water comes out with submersible pumps and hoses, down to the footing trenches and dips where it gathers. Any silt or mud sitting on the liner gets removed during this step.

3

Remove Wet Materials

We bag up the wet insulation, damaged vapor barrier and loose debris and take it all outside. Where mold has begun or is likely, the bare joists and subfloor get cleaned and an antimicrobial applied.

4

Final Drying and Checks

Low profile air movers and a dehumidifier run under the house until the wood reaches a normal moisture reading. A final meter check follows, and you receive the numbers along with steps to prevent a repeat.
