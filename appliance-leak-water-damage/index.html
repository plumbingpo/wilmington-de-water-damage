Dealing With Appliance Leak Water Damage in Wilmington

Most of the appliance leaks we see trace back to three machines. When the supply hose on a washing machine splits, water flows under full pressure until somebody turns it off. A dishwasher tends to leak slowly from a door seal or drain hose, out of sight under the cabinets. Behind a refrigerator, the thin tube feeding the ice maker may drip for months. No two of these call for the same cleanup.

For homeowners in Wilmington, we begin by sliding the machine out to see how far the water traveled. In kitchens that usually means under the cabinets and beneath the flooring. Laundry room water goes into the wall at the back of the machine and the neighboring rooms, and with an upstairs laundry, the ceiling underneath. Standing water is extracted, absorbed moisture is measured, and drying equipment is sized to fit the wet area.

Right Now

Your First Steps

  1. Turn Off the ApplianceCancel the cycle, and pull the plug only when the floor and the outlet near it are dry. When either one is wet, turn the power off at the breaker.
  2. Close the Supply ValveWashers have hot and cold valves on the wall behind them. The dishwasher valve is under the kitchen sink. The small valve for an ice maker line sits behind the refrigerator or underneath.
  3. Mop Up What You CanGetting reachable water up with a mop or towels stops it from moving under the cabinets and the floor. Do not vacuum unless the machine is rated for wet and dry use.
  4. Keep the Door ClosedIf the washer or dishwasher has not drained, leave it shut. The entire load of water may spill across the floor if the door is opened mid-cycle.
  5. Photograph the Failed PartPhotograph the failed fitting, valve or hose, and hold on to the part itself. Your insurer and the appliance maker may both ask to see it.
Insurance

What About Insurance?

Homeowners policies often cover sudden and accidental events, and a burst hose or a machine overflowing with no warning is that kind of event. What is typically paid is the water damage, while the failed part and the appliance are left out. Slow seepage from a dishwasher or ice maker line is a harder claim, because gradual leaks usually are not covered. Certain insurers will want to know the hose's age and whether it was kept up. Hold on to the part that failed, photograph everything ahead of cleanup, and review what your policy says.

The Usual Culprits

How It Usually Starts

  • A dishwasher with a failed pump seal, a cracked drain hose or a worn door gasket.
  • A clogged defrost drain or cracked drain pan that lets a refrigerator leak onto the floor.
  • Inlet valves that stick open, so the dishwasher or washer keeps taking in water past the fill level.
  • Rubber washing machine supply hoses that weaken with age and split under pressure.
  • Ice maker lines, copper or plastic, that crack, kink or come loose where they connect.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Hidden Mold at the Back

The warm, dark space behind a refrigerator or dishwasher rarely gets checked. Long before it is discovered, a slow drip back there can be growing mold across the floor and wall.

Adjoining Rooms

Water may pour from a washer hose for hours on end. That water moves beneath walls into bedrooms, closets and hallways, soaking pad and carpet far from the laundry room.

Flooring and Subfloor

Water slides beneath laminate, vinyl plank and hardwood. The boards swell or cup, while the subfloor below remains wet well after the top looks dry.

Wall Bases and Trim

Water standing on a laundry or kitchen floor wicks up into baseboards and the bottom of the drywall. Since it is out of view, the wall behind the machine tends to be wetter than the rest.

Our Process

Our Appliance Leak Water Damage Process

1

Move the Appliance and Inspect

We disconnect and move the appliance, confirm the supply is off, and trace the water path under cabinets, beneath flooring and into nearby walls with moisture meters and thermal imaging.

2

Extract the Water

Standing water is extracted from floors and carpet. To reach water caught below cabinets or under floating floors, our crew pulls toe kicks or takes up boards.

3

Dry in Place

Dehumidifiers and air movers are set up, and when it is possible, directed airflow dries hardwood and cabinets where they sit so nothing has to be torn out.

4

Sanitize and Take Final Readings

Any surface that dishwasher or washer discharge reached is treated with an antimicrobial. Before the appliance is put back, a last set of readings confirms the floor under it is no longer damp.
