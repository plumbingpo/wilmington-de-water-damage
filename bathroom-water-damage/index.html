Bathroom Water Damage Help in Wilmington

A bathroom is designed to handle water on its surfaces, never below them. When caulk cracks, grout wears thin or a toilet seal fails, a little water slips past with every shower and every flush. It collects in the subfloor and the wall behind the tile, out of sight. In Wilmington, we locate the entry point, measure how far the water has spread, then dry or take out the wet material.

These leaks are usually slow, so the damage is often deeper and older than it appears. Subfloor under a toilet can be soft all the way through while the tile above still looks fine. With thermal imaging and moisture meters, we map what is wet before any demolition, and we open just the sections that will not dry in place.

What It Looks Like

  • A musty smell that comes back no matter how often the room is cleaned
  • Floor tiles that sound hollow, rock underfoot, or have come loose near the tub
  • Edges of the vinyl floor peeling up, or dark discoloration showing through it
  • A warped or swollen cabinet floor under the vanity sink
  • The caulk line at the shower or tub has split, pulled away, or turned black
  • Slight rocking when you sit on the toilet, or a dark ring on the floor around it
Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Mold in the Tiled Wall

By themselves, grout and tile do not keep water out. Mold takes hold on the wall board once that hidden layer remains wet, and no scrubbing can get to it.

Slow Vanity Drips

Behind the towels and bottles kept under the sink, a drain fitting or supply valve can drip for months. First the bottom of the cabinet swells, and then the wall behind and the subfloor get wet.

Poor Ventilation

Steam lingers after each shower when the exhaust fan is weak or absent. That moisture settles on walls and ceilings, feeds mold, and slows the drying of anything that is already wet.

Leaking Shower Pan

With a crack in the liner or pan, water escapes below the shower floor at each use. The surface looks perfect while the framing below stays soaked.

How We Fix It

How We Handle Bathroom Water Damage

1

Pinpoint the Leak

Each fixture is tested separately, from the supply lines to the toilet, tub and shower, and thermal imaging and moisture meters show us where water went beneath tile and in the walls.

2

Open What Is Needed

We take out rotted subfloor, soft wall board and tile that has come loose. A toilet leak means contamination, so anything porous that will not come clean is removed.

3

Dry the Cavities

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the subfloor, the wall framing and the ceiling space below. Before anything gets closed up, antimicrobial is applied and dry readings are confirmed.

4

Rebuild and Reseal

We replace subfloor and backer board, and can coordinate tile, a new toilet seal, fresh caulk and ceiling repair so the leak path is closed, not covered over.

Why Speed Matters

The Damage Timeline

The first few days

Water Slips Through

Each shower pushes a little water through the failed caulk or grout. Backer board and the outer rim of the subfloor take it in, and there is still nothing to see.

The first several weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

The wood under the tile never gets a chance to dry between showers. Mold starts on the underside of the floor and inside the wall cavity.

One to three months

Signs Start to Appear

Grout darkens, tiles loosen, and the caulk line turns black. Where a room sits underneath, its ceiling develops a stain that gradually spreads.

Six months and beyond

Structure Weakens

Joists can begin to rot, and the subfloor near the toilet or tub becomes soft. At this point the job means new tile and carpentry on top of the drying.
